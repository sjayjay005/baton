Privacy Policy

Baton – Dev & QA Toolkit · last updated 10 November 2026 (v0.5.1)

Baton is a Chrome extension that helps developers and QA engineers inspect, mock and replay what a web page does, and turn that into a bug report. This policy explains what Baton does with your data.

The short version: Baton has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no server of its own. Everything it records stays in your browser on your computer until you delete it or choose to export it.

What Baton does not do

  • Baton does not collect, store, sell or share personal information.
  • Baton has no backend: there is nowhere for your data to be uploaded to.
  • Baton contains no analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or advertising code.
  • Baton does not track your browsing history or send your page content anywhere.

What Baton stores, and where

All of it is local to your browser profile, in storage that belongs to this extension only:

Data Where it lives
Settings, mock rules, team links, storage snapshots, saved console snippets, your Azure DevOps personal access token and your GIPHY API key chrome.storage.local
Screenshots, screen recordings and GIFs you capture IndexedDB, until you delete them
Captured requests, responses, console output, the timeline and recorded replay sessions In the DevTools panel for the current session, plus any session you explicitly save or export

Uninstalling Baton removes this data with it. Stored values are not encrypted, so on a shared machine use Forget PAT in Settings to clear your Azure token.

Network connections

Baton itself makes network requests to exactly two third-party services, and only when you ask it to:

  • dev.azure.com – when you connect Azure Boards or click Create Bug. Your personal access token, the bug report you wrote and any attachments you included are sent to your own Azure DevOps organisation, under Microsoft's handling of that service.
  • api.giphy.com – when you search for a GIF. Your search term is sent to GIPHY under their privacy policy.

Nothing else leaves your browser. Replaying a request sends it to that request's own server, exactly as the page would have.

Page content and recordings

To do its job, Baton observes the page you have DevTools open on: requests and their bodies, console output, storage and cookie changes, and your clicks, typing and navigation. This is processed in your browser and kept locally as described above.

Baton excludes password and secret input fields from recordings. With Mask secrets on, tokens in storage and auth cookies are redacted in anything you export, and auth headers in a Bruno export become variables you fill in yourself.

Exported files — .baton.json, .baton-session.json, Markdown reports, Bruno collections, screenshots and recordings — are written to your computer by you. Baton does not upload them. Be aware that an export can contain whatever the page showed, including data from your application, so share it the way you would share any other debugging artefact.

Children

Baton is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13.
